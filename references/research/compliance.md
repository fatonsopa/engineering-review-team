# Compliance research and coverage

Primary sources checked on 13 November 2026. This record maps the requested scope to fourteen controls in the [Compliance Technical Standard (STD)](../standards/compliance.md). It supplies technical review guidance, not a determination of legal applicability, certification, or release approval. The checks and examples below are engineering interpretations of the cited sources; they are not quotations or observations about a real product. Source versions and research limitations belong here, outside the routinely loaded reviewer instructions.

## Coverage of requested responsibilities

The supplied minimum scope covers open-source license obligations, sensitive-data handling and privacy choices, required corporate headers, and audit records for permission/tracking changes. The agreed team scope adds provenance, data lifecycle, holds, recipients, residency and evidence against established obligations. The [procedure](../domains/compliance.md) owns investigation; the [Policy (POL)](../policies/compliance.md) owns review obligations. Shared team guidance owns discovery, authority and reporting.

## Control support and examples

- **CMP-01 — Establish applicable obligations.** Identify the reviewed activity, data, organizational role, intended recipients, geography and distribution or service model. Bind each applicable obligation to its actual license, contract, internal rule or legal source and version. GDPR territorial scope is not a citizenship test; HIPAA depends on covered-entity/business-associate roles; PCI DSS concerns payment-data and relevant security scope. Record unresolved material applicability with the responsible owner. Example: a small internal arithmetic utility with no relevant data or third-party material need not gain consent screens or a PCI checklist. Its size alone would not exempt a utility that actually handles relevant data. Sources: S01, S11, S16, S21.

- **CMP-02 — Establish provenance and an accurate inventory.** Inspect new or changed direct/transitive dependencies, vendored files, copied snippets, generated deliverables and relevant fonts, media or datasets. Connect exact versions or revisions and origin to license text, exceptions, selected alternatives and actual inclusion in deliverables. SPDX expressions distinguish alternatives (`OR`), cumulative obligations (`AND`) and exceptions (`WITH`); preserve those distinctions. A scanner or inventory is evidence to reconcile with artifacts, not proof of a license grant or infringement. Example: an unclassified copied module needs provenance resolution; a declared dual-license option legitimately selected and satisfied is not automatically a copyleft defect. Sources: S02–03, S08.

- **CMP-03 — Check the obligations triggered by actual use.** Determine whether the change conveys software, modifies covered files, forms a covered combined work or exposes a modified network service under the relevant terms. Inspect required corresponding source, recipient access, offers and installation information where applicable. GPLv3 distinguishes conveyance from mere network interaction; AGPLv3 §13 has a modified-program network-source condition; MPL 2.0 permits larger works under other terms while retaining obligations for covered software. Example: shipping a covered binary without its required source route can be defective; compliant use of a copyleft component is not a blanket prohibition. Escalate disputed linkage or compatibility interpretation. Sources: S04–06.

- **CMP-04 — Preserve notices and truthful ownership.** Check license copies, relevant copyright/attribution notices, modification notices and any required NOTICE content in the actual released package, including minified or bundled output. Apply corporate headers only where the current internal rule requires them, and distinguish original company work from third-party material. Example: retaining MIT notices only in the development checkout while omitting them from distributed copies can fail; replacing a corporate header convention may be valid while removing upstream notices is still defective. Apache 2.0 does not require inventing an upstream NOTICE file that never existed. REUSE is an optional metadata convention, not universal law. Sources: S03, S07–10.

- **CMP-05 — Trace data and purposes.** For affected processing, locate collection points, subjects, categories, purposes, recipients, stores and responsible roles. Include identifiers, free text, telemetry, SDKs, caches, derived data and backups where relevant; a field named `anonymousId` does not establish anonymity. Compare new collection or reuse with the established purpose and lawful basis or permitted use. Example: sending support text to a new analytics service without reconciling its purpose and recipient is a review concern; a minimal local setting with no external processing does not require a fabricated processor contract. Sources: S12–13, S18.

- **CMP-06 — Minimize data and protect sensitive outputs.** Check that collection, logging, error reports, traces, exports and diagnostic dumps expose only the data permitted for their purpose. Exercise or inspect failure paths as well as successful display code. Distinguish display masking, protected storage, irreversible removal and de-identification. Example: a masked payment screen does not excuse full PAN in logs; a hashed patient identifier is not automatically HIPAA de-identified. HHS recognizes Expert Determination and Safe Harbor with conditions. Required audit evidence should be minimized and protected rather than indiscriminately suppressed. Sources: S12, S17, S23.

- **CMP-07 — Honor required choices throughout processing.** Identify the required choice and its purpose: consent, withdrawal, objection, tracking preference or another established control. Check initial defaults, affirmative action where required, authoritative saved state, UI/API agreement, SDK initialization, background jobs and retries. Check that withdrawal or changed preference reaches relevant processing and that evidence identifies the choice and applicable notice/version when needed. Example: disabling a toggle while a worker keeps sending optional analytics contradicts the choice; retaining necessary billing records after marketing withdrawal can be valid. Consent is one lawful basis, not a universal prerequisite. Sources: S13, S14; project-specific audit evidence also maps to CMP-11.

- **CMP-08 — Enforce retention and legitimate holds.** Map each affected data category to its established retention trigger, duration, deletion method and hold authority. Inspect scheduled deletion, object versions, derived stores, vendor copies, failed jobs and restore behavior; distinguish active deletion from documented backup expiry and restricted restoration. Holds should have a justified scope, access restrictions and release behavior, not become an indefinite blanket exemption. Example: a successful delete response with a failed deletion queue is defective; a documented applicable hold may justify restricted preservation. HIPAA's six-year documentation rule in §164.316 is not a universal six-year rule for all medical records. Sources: S12, S14, S19.

- **CMP-09 — Support applicable rights and exports.** Trace access, correction, erasure, restriction and portability duties that actually apply to the processing and role. Verify proportionate identity checks, subject/tenant boundaries, relevant stores and recipients, response handling, completion evidence and applicable exceptions. Access and portability are different rights; GDPR portability has consent/contract and automated-processing conditions. Example: a cross-tenant export is defective even if its format is correct; a recorded lawful exception may make partial erasure appropriate. Do not report full deletion or export when only one store was checked. An API endpoint for every right is not a universal implementation requirement. Sources: S01, S14, S18.

- **CMP-10 — Match processors, transfers and residency commitments.** Compare actual vendors, purposes, subprocessors, support access, endpoints, failover, logs and backups with the executed agreement and approved processing instructions. Identify whether an international-transfer mechanism or contractual residency restriction applies, and whether the implementation respects it. GDPR does not impose a universal EU-only hosting rule; region selection alone cannot establish the legality of a transfer or control remote access. Example: failover outside an expressly contracted region contradicts that contract; a permitted transfer with the applicable arrangements need not be rejected merely because it crosses a border. Sources: S01, S15, S18, S22.

- **CMP-11 — Produce usable, proportionate audit evidence.** For the established audit scope, explicitly inspect permission changes and tracking-choice changes. Match an operation to its record: actor or service, affected subject/resource, action, time, outcome and sufficient correlation or state/version context. Check persistence, retry/duplicate behavior, rollback consistency, access restrictions, alteration protection, retention and authorized evidence retrieval according to the obligation. Example: recording success before a failed permission transaction, or dropping a required preference event, can defeat auditability. These fields are engineering recommendations; no cited source universally mandates this exact event schema or immutable indefinite logging. HIPAA §164.312(b) requires mechanisms to record and examine relevant ePHI system activity. Sources: S13, S16, S19, plus the actual project audit rule.

- **CMP-12 — Connect obligations to security and incident mechanisms.** Identify technical safeguards and incident handling demanded by applicable sources or contracts, then trace them to actual access, transmission, storage and response mechanisms. A document claiming encryption or access control does not demonstrate its implementation. Bring evidence to the Security Reviewer for threat, authorization and cryptographic depth, and DevOps Reviewer for operational response. Example: a contractually required incident escalation with no implementable trigger/contact is a gap; the Compliance Reviewer should not independently notify regulators or declare an incident legally reportable. HIPAA's distinction between required and addressable specifications must not be rewritten as blanket optionality. Sources: S16, S18.

- **CMP-13 — Apply named frameworks precisely.** Map only established, relevant clauses to observable controls; record organization, role, service and version limits. For a merchant, PCI SSC states that post-authorization card-verification-code storage is prohibited even if encrypted or customer-approved. Outsourced payments may reduce scope while leaving merchant responsibilities. Conversely, HIPAA is not triggered by every health-related application. Example: retaining CVV after authorization is a concrete scoped concern; absence of a generic 'HIPAA opt-out' is not. A handful of passing code checks cannot establish full GDPR, HIPAA or PCI DSS compliance. Sources: S11, S20–24.

- **CMP-14 — Bind evidence, decisions and claims.** State the exact obligation, current authority, artifact or operation, observed behavior, evidence location, uncertainty and consequence. Separate a code contradiction from missing applicability information, inaccessible evidence, approved internal deviation and untested behavior. Preserve original decisions and expiry dates; an expired internal audit waiver cannot silently authorize continued omission. A complete authorized internal replacement may displace internal defaults within its scope, while applicable third-party license, legal and contractual obligations remain. Give authorized reviewers sufficient evidence without disclosing unnecessary personal data. Approval, a scan or a model assertion is not certification. Sources: S04–07, S19, S24; internal authority behavior derives from the local agreed scope.

## Responsibility and evidence limits

The Compliance Reviewer maps established obligations to technical behavior and reports supported discrepancies. Legal/privacy owners resolve disputed jurisdiction, legal basis, copyright ownership, license compatibility and contract interpretation. The Security Reviewer owns detailed abuse, authorization and cryptographic analysis; the Database Reviewer owns storage and migration depth; the DevOps Reviewer owns operational durability, access and response evidence; the Testing Reviewer assesses verification quality. These boundaries do not excuse a visible compliance defect or require needless delegation for a straightforward check.

The research does not determine a reviewed project's actual regulated status or invent contracts, approved exceptions, data inventories or completed tests. Missing internal guidance is not proof that law or third-party terms do not apply; it also does not justify inventing a corporate rule. Use bounded evidence gaps and conditional conclusions where authority is unresolved.

Coverage intentionally excludes a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal survey, tax/medical retention schedules, children's rules, sector-specific consent laws, advertising cookie regimes, export-control analysis, patent clearance and a complete GDPR/HIPAA/PCI audit. Those require actual applicability and competent owners before adding controls. No proposed regulation was substituted for a final rule. GPL/AGPL and EUR-Lex access limitations and the inaccessible full PCI PDF are disclosed above; the narrower retrieved primary provisions support the concrete examples, not a claim of exhaustive legal review.
